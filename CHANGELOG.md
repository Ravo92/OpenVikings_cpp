# OpenVikings Remaster Changelog

Daily summaries of the final changes on `remaster` and its separate `network` development branch. Each date has one consolidated entry; times are local to Vienna, Austria (CEST, UTC+02:00).

## 2026-10-21 · 12:38 CEST

AI villages prioritize staffing, families and defense before further expansion. Autonomous construction is limited to two farms, mills, bakeries, wells and beehives per player, counting upgrades and construction sites. Existing production takes priority over new buildings, construction crews are reduced when workers are needed elsewhere, and warehouse expansion depends on workforce and defense. Raw-material collection targets two collectors per needed resource, with collection flags and warehouse carriers; qualified collectors can advance into crafts. Bread production, housing for homeless families and more frequent family planning support population growth while retaining food costs and population limits. Soldiers are recruited from surplus workers without disrupting production, logistics or existing troops. All supported civilizations use the original well and beehive models with their normal water and honey production; civilization building choices use named house-type enums. Soldiers with unsupported weapon appearances use their civilization's original figures and complete native animations without changing their weapons or combat values. Saracen woodcutters retain a complete head animation during axe swings, and clay carried by Byzantine inhabitants uses its clay material colors. Egyptian settlements use the AddOn Arabs2 Standard, Wealthy and Danger music set. Obstructed falling trees, including abandoned trees in older saves, recover without replaying their falling animation: if the trunk cannot fit, the full wood quantity moves to a nearby safe pile and the tree disappears. When no safe position is available, the tree retains its final animation frame until placement becomes possible, preserving its wood.

## 2026-10-20 · 23:43 CEST

Single-player map selection separates Missions from Skirmish while retaining tutorials, campaigns and saved games. Skirmish combines a wide, scrollable map list with a centered preview, dimensions beside the Map preview heading, framed descriptions and difficulty settings. Translucent panels and the original menu font replace large opaque brown fills; redundant headings, player-name columns and nested description frames are removed. Narrower civilization fields leave more room for map names, the fill button sits at the bottom of the player panel, and fixed map colors remain fully visible with an explanation of their restrictions. Skirmish and the multiplayer lobby use padded control and civilization dropdowns with separate arrows, mouse and keyboard navigation, and Computer labels. Connected players are assigned automatically; available slots can be filled with computer players, and local skirmish allows choosing the human start position with difficulty benefits applied to that slot. Map restrictions and existing save assignments are retained. Non-Viking computer players use civilization-specific starting buildings and economic and military production chains, reusing suitable native models for missing building purposes. Egyptian large warehouses participate in resource delivery and settlement planning, and session building profiles are restored from new saves. Visible buildings across civilizations display localized purpose names on hover and in the General panel. Computer players depend on natural resources, production and transport: periodic five-unit stock gifts and free initial warehouse construction goods are removed. Idle unarmed soldiers collect suitable available weapons through normal equipment handling without replacing active orders. The main-menu village is protected from automatic demolition. Multiplayer synchronization adapts automatically to measured latency and variation while preserving simulation speed; its window shows per-player latency graphs, RTT, variation, connection status and the current cycle mode. Startup carries civilization choices consistently, address lists handle IPv6 and long status text safely, transfer cancellation is acknowledged, and send-queue failures are reported without disconnecting healthy forwarding peers. Participants need matching updated network builds. Offline skirmish, shared player settings and automatic synchronization policy have separate implementation responsibilities. Die Saga gains its own launcher, resource profile, campaign artwork and progress markers for Nordland, Wunder and Saga, plus both user-campaign menus. User campaigns load from original archives or unpacked folders, using one entry per map GUID and preferring the folder when both forms exist; this game profile remains experimental.

## 2026-10-19 · 23:56 CEST

UTF-8 text supports Unicode keyboard, IME and clipboard input, character-safe editing, savegame-editor names and map text, with DirectWrite shaping and Windows font fallback for characters absent from the original fonts. Fallback text renders upright; coverage depends on installed fonts, and translations are not included. The network branch preserves Unicode player and session names, chat and rename records. Cultures 2 uses its own menu scenery and text tables, animal selection, music directory and campaign identity, with the ten mission buttons and unlocked map sections tied to campaign progress. Selected maps start directly without the later games' difficulty screen. Compressed IMA-ADPCM effects retain their recorded lengths and play at the original 44,100 Hz rate. The Extras page uses Cultures 2's Letters and Extras for everyone entries, including the four original assistance options; unsupported difficulty text is omitted from its options window. BIK intro and mission videos play through the installation's `binkw32.dll`, and the original framed loading bar uses neutral grey shading and a moving highlight. Isolated red speckles in the enlarged minimap frame blend into its brown shading. Shared startup handling makes the first loading window visible in the taskbar and restores normal foreground activation and input focus. Campaign selection keeps each game's own campaign identities.

## 2026-10-18 · 18:45 CEST

The separate `network` branch replaces DirectPlay with an experimental native Windows TCP/IP transport integrated with the existing multiplayer menu and game synchronization. It supports IPv4 LAN discovery and direct hostname, IPv4 or IPv6 discovery, reliable ordered messages, bounded packets, timeouts and host-controlled admission. Map and save transfers verify downloaded data before replacing files and acknowledge completion; command backups, text conversion, log formatting and transfer progress are corrected. All participants need matching builds; original Cultures multiplayer, host migration and public matchmaking are outside this implementation. Game launchers share one static engine library and common build settings, with separate project intermediates and symbols. Northland has a prepared launcher; its data loading remains unimplemented. DirectMusic initializes and processes loading and playback on a dedicated thread, retaining queued commands and volume settings while startup continues. A standalone savegame editor edits villager names, needs, health, equipment, experience, building inventories and save information, with undo, redo and JSON export while preserving source saves and unknown data. Starting-map export writes terrain, graphics, fish, players, buildings, stock, inhabitants, animals, vehicles and signposts with native placement masks, pathfinding data and compressed DAT planes. Saved permanent restrictions remain distinct from unlockable progression; mission definitions, mission progress, scripted AI and exact runtime state are not exported. Archive tables, paths, map/save packing, clipboard access and audio failure paths receive bounds and ownership corrections; unknown audio is skipped, rejected villager creation retains free slots, RGB555 blending is corrected, and recovery and refresh timers handle clock wrap. Invalid walking routes are rejected before accessing route buffers or map data. Builders are recruited only for reachable work points on the same landmass, with connected signposts required for longer automatic journeys. Workers follow signposts in the correct direction and retain their destination, fixing repeated failures to deliver workshop materials. AI attack groups retry reachable approach positions without overwriting the new movement order, restoring stalled northern attack waves while retaining their attack areas, timers and troop requirements. Both fullscreen and windowed startup use the Windows primary monitor.

## 2026-10-17 · 03:35 CEST

Computer villages harvest nearby deposits at collection flags independently of warehouse stock, and dedicated warehouse carriers bring the goods back. Collection sites remain usable after starting supplies run out, pause when storage is full and resume when capacity returns, using normal harvesting, hauling and pathfinding. Viking wooden-spear soldiers have complete resting animations at the intended pace without changing sleep duration or stamina recovery. Vehicle action buttons use the existing localized labels and help text for passenger disembarkation, unloading and docking. The Viking stonemason's work and carrying animations keep rubble, stone blocks and marble in their intended stone colors. Brick production displays the potter's oven fire, and cake production shows the baker's complete exit, work and entry sequence with cake retained through the final carrying step. Production durations and the working bread sequence are preserved.

## 2026-10-16 · 20:48 CEST

DirectX 11 presentation adds borderless fullscreen, mouse confinement, Alt+Enter switching and resizable windows without repeated desktop-resolution changes. Resolution choices use sorted 32-bit modes, game aspect ratios are preserved with letterboxing, and menus, HUD elements, cursors and loading bars scale with the display. The enlarged minimap has corrected framing, transparency, shadow and spacing; fullscreen videos and cursor presentation are improved. Rain, snow and sandstorms render through the 32-bit graphics path with existing simulation, ordering, detail and zoom behavior. Supported Microsoft C++ runtime and Windows services replace the reconstructed legacy runtime, custom heaps, private compiler and math backends, and x87 bridges while retaining Win32 and existing game-data formats. SSE2 replaces remaining MMX sprite and terrain rendering with the same integer rounding, saturation and HUD transparency rules; obsolete MMX detection, 16-bit output paths and the unused DirectInput mouse backend are removed. Multiplayer maps launched solo assign the other active human start positions to computer control while preserving the local player, saved assignments, diplomacy and scripted AI. School training awards the selected skill, equipment unlocks and permissions use their own definitions, and military profession changes update temporarily detached inhabitants. Water navigation uses the correct sector counter. Vehicle stock and reservation handling respect capacities, reject full reservation lists without side effects, release promises after failed routes or door searches, and handle failed food pickup or delivery safely; workers can leave completed vehicle construction tasks. Military targeting accepts the affected 19-to-30-point range while retaining weapon minimum ranges. Palette brightness, empty minimap samples, caption parsing, help links, text loading and map-edge markers receive bounds and initialization fixes, and off-map projectiles release their slots. WAV loading follows RIFF chunks and padding, rejects truncated input and advances memory-backed read cursors correctly. Sound creation and reload release temporary resources and avoid duplicate entries; unknown jingles are skipped and failed buffer restoration returns instead of retrying indefinitely. Volume transitions start from the current level, reach their targets, handle zero duration and muting, preserve relative volume and stereo position, and stop playback when fading to zero. Archive fallback respects the selected archive and search-path settings, completed directory searches terminate normally, and invalid building, vehicle, civilization and experience definitions are rejected before they can overwrite neighboring data.
